The Apothecary Diaries

Scroll ↓

Sold into the rear palace, she counts the days by what grows in the yard.

Maomao was an apothecary's daughter before she was anything else. She was kidnapped and put to work as a low-ranking servant behind the palace walls, she keeps her head down until a string of unexplained illnesses among the emperor's infant children gives her a reason to notice everything.

A eunuch official notices the girl who reads poison like handwriting.

Jinshi, elegant and irritatingly perceptive, starts finding reasons to call on her. Maomao would rather be left to her herbs, but somehow the cases keep finding her, one strange symptom at a time.

Every court intrigue starts the same way: something small doesn't add up.

A dish tastes faintly of almonds. A concubine's skin pales in a pattern no physician names. To everyone else it looks like fate, or curse, or coincidence. Maomao reads it as chemistry, which in turn leaves a trail.

The same flower that kills, in the right hand, becomes a cure.

Lily bulbs, wolfsbane, mercury in face powder; the rear palace is a garden of things that can end a life or save one, depending on who is holding the dose. Maomao's gift isn't magic. It's patience, and a willingness to taste what no one else will touch.

She solves the mystery not for praise, but because leaving it unsolved would bother her more.

That's the whole of her motive, and it's enough. Curiosity, applied carefully, turns out to be its own kind of courage — quieter than a sword, just as sharp.

A blade between her teeth, a diagnosis already forming and she steps back into the palace.

Freckled and entirely herself. Maomao was never going to be the palace's most beautiful woman. She settles for being its most dangerous mind.
